About the Book

Two civilizations that never met each wrote a manual for the same journey.

The Egyptian Book of the Dead and the Tibetan Book of the Dead (the Bardo Thodol) both describe what waits for the soul after death: the passages, the judgment, the lights, and the beings met along the way.

The Third Book of the Dead reads these two ancient texts side by side. Chapter by chapter it reveals their striking overlaps, then brings in a witness nobody expected: modern ufology.

Through Hermeticism, Gnosticism, and Kabbalah, the book builds toward one coherent picture of what this reality actually is, what happens after death, and what life is for.

It includes a genuine revelation about the true name of Jesus, pieced together from ancient sources in a way that has not been presented before. Beyond theory, it offers practical ways to apply these systems and glimpses into the mechanism behind manifestation.

This is not a book for everyone. If it has found its way to you, read on.

About the Author

Farel Jasin Abba is the author of The Third Book of the Dead.

The work arose from years of close reading of the Egyptian and Tibetan books of the dead, placed in dialogue with the broader Western occult tradition (Hermeticism, Gnosticism, Kabbalah) and contemporary accounts from the field of ufology. The goal was not to produce another summary of these texts, but to follow their internal logic until a single, coherent picture of the afterlife and the nature of reality emerged — including material that has not been assembled in this way before.

The book is written for readers who are willing to look carefully at the sources and follow the argument themselves.

Why This Book Was Written

The Egyptian Book of the Dead and the Tibetan Bardo Thodol describe the same journey in different languages and symbols. Most readers treat them as separate cultural curiosities. This book asks what happens when they are read together, and when the testimony of modern ufology is allowed into the conversation.

The result is a single picture: of the passages after death, of the nature of the beings encountered, of the purpose of life in this reality, and of a long-hidden name associated with Jesus that emerges once the sources are aligned.

It was written for those who sense that the old manuals still have something precise to say, and who are open to following the evidence wherever it leads.
